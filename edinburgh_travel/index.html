Edinburgh Travel

Getting around Edinburgh without the stress.

Edinburgh is a walkable city, but between the hills, the cobbles, and the distance from the airport, you'll want to know your options. Here's a plain-English rundown of trams, buses, Voi bikes, the Edinburgh City Pass, and the open-top tour buses — plus exactly how to get in from the airport.

Trams

Edinburgh's tram line runs in a single line from Edinburgh Airport in the west, through Haymarket and along Princes Street, out to Newhaven on the waterfront in the east. It's modern, step-free, and by far the calmest way to travel with luggage — no traffic lights to fight, just a smooth run on dedicated track.

Trams run roughly every 7–10 minutes at peak times and a little less often in the evening. You buy a ticket from the machine on the platform (or tap a contactless card/phone directly on board) — there are no ticket barriers, but inspectors do check, so always validate before you travel.

Route: Airport → Newhaven Frequency: ~7–10 min Best for: Airport transfers, waterfront/Leith
Good to know: the tram does not stop right at Waverley Station — the closest stop is Princes Street/St Andrew Square, about a 10-minute walk away.
🚌

Lothian Buses

Lothian Buses run the city's everyday bus network and are the backbone of getting around — dozens of routes reach almost everywhere, including areas the tram doesn't touch, like the Old Town closes, Stockbridge, and the southside.

Fares are flat regardless of distance, and the driver won't give change, so have the exact fare or — much easier — just tap a contactless card or phone as you board. Lothian's daily and weekly caps mean you'll never pay more than the cap even if you hop on and off all day.

Coverage: City-wide network Payment: Contactless tap-on Best for: Reaching anywhere off the tram line
Tip: download the free Lothian Buses app for live arrival times and to buy tickets in advance if you'd rather not tap a card.
🛴

Voi Bikes

Voi operates Edinburgh's dockless e-bike share scheme. Pick one up with the app, ride it, and leave it in a designated parking zone near your destination — no need to return it to where you started.

They're a fun way to cover ground quickly, especially along the flatter routes like the canal towpath or the waterfront to Leith.
Worth knowing before you hop on: consider buying the monthly Voi Pass, so you can reserve you bike up to 30 minutes ahead and not pay the unlock fees. This pays for itself after only six rides!

Type: Dockless e-bike / e-scooter Pricing: Unlock fee + per-minute Best for: Short hops, flat waterfront routes
Tip: scooters must be parked in marked bays shown in the app — leaving one blocking a pavement can land you a fine, so always check the in-app map before ending your ride.
🎟️

Edinburgh City Pass

The Edinburgh City Pass bundles free or discounted entry to major attractions — think Edinburgh Castle, the Royal Yacht Britannia, and various tours — into a single ticket, usually alongside unlimited travel on the hop-on hop-off open-top buses for the days it covers.

It's worth doing the maths before buying: add up what you'd actually pay for the attractions on your list plus a day or two of open-top bus travel, then compare that to the pass price. For a packed few days of sightseeing it can save real money and hassle; for a slower, one-or-two-sight trip you may be better off paying individually.

Includes: Attraction entry + bus tours Formats: 1, 2, or 3-day options Best for: Attraction-heavy itineraries
Tip: book popular attractions like the Castle in advance even with a pass — it reserves your slot rather than guaranteeing walk-up entry.
🚍

Open-Top Bus Tours

The classic hop-on hop-off open-top buses loop past all the big sights — the Castle, Royal Mile, Holyrood Palace, the Grassmarket, and more — with recorded commentary and a stop roughly every 10–15 minutes. Buy a ticket (typically valid 24 or 48 hours) and hop off wherever catches your eye, then catch the next bus onward whenever you're ready.

They're less about efficient point-to-point travel and more about orientation — a good first move on day one to get your bearings, see everything from above street level, and decide what's worth coming back to on foot.

Ticket: 24 / 48 hr hop-on hop-off Stops: Every ~10–15 min Best for: First-day orientation, top sights
Tip: sit upstairs at the front if the weather's playing along — bring a layer even in summer, Edinburgh wind doesn't care about the forecast.

✈️ Airport ↔ City Centre: the full route

Two easy ways in — the tram and the Airlink 100 bus. Here's exactly how each one works, in both directions.

🚊

By Tram

  1. Follow signs for Trams from the terminal — the stop is a short walk outside arrivals.
  2. Buy a single or return ticket from the platform machine, or just tap a contactless card/phone as you board.
  3. Ride toward York Place / Newhaven.
  4. Get off at Princes Street or St Andrew Square for the city centre (roughly 25–30 minutes from the airport).
~25–30 min Every 7–10 min No stop at Waverley — 10 min walk
🚌

By Airlink 100 Bus

  1. Follow signs for Buses just outside the terminal — the Airlink stop is clearly marked.
  2. Buy on board with contactless, or pre-book via the Bus & Tram app.
  3. The express bus runs non-stop-ish, with only a couple of stops (Haymarket, Princes Street) en route.
  4. Get off at Waverley Bridge, right next to Waverley Station, for the city centre.
~25–35 min Every 10 min (20 min overnight) Runs 24 hours
— and the reverse trip —
🔁

City Centre → Airport

Same two options, just run in reverse:

  1. Tram: catch it heading westbound at Princes Street or St Andrew Square and ride to the Airport terminus — it's the last stop, so you can't miss it.
  2. Airlink 100: pick it up at Waverley Bridge (or Haymarket, or Princes Street) heading toward the airport — again, terminus stop, so just stay on until the end of the line.
Which one should you take? The tram is smoother and better for heavy luggage, but doesn't reach Waverley Station directly. The Airlink bus drops you right at Waverley and runs all night, making it the better call for very early or very late flights. Traffic can occasionally slow the bus more than the tram, which runs on its own dedicated track.
